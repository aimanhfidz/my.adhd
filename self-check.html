The self-check.

Ten minutes, no login, no cost.

This is the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1) — the screening questionnaire used by health services, reproduced here in full. Answer honestly about the past six months and you’ll get a clear read on whether your traits fall within the range that warrants a proper assessment.

  • Questions6, or 18 if you want the fuller picture
  • TimeAbout 5 minutes
  • CostFree, and no account
  • Your answersNever stored, never sent
  • What you getA score out of 6, and what it means

Who wrote these questions

The eighteen questions in this self-check are the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1) Symptom Checklist. They are reproduced word for word; MyADHD Malaysia did not write them and has not altered them.

The checklist was developed in conjunction with the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Workgroup on Adult ADHD, which included:

  • Lenard Adler, MD — Associate Professor of Psychiatry and Neurology, New York University Medical School
  • Ronald C. Kessler, PhD — Professor, Department of Health Care Policy, Harvard Medical School
  • Thomas Spencer, MD — Associate Professor of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical School

The questions are consistent with DSM-IV criteria and address how ADHD symptoms show up in adults. The scoring used here is the instrument’s own: Part A is the six-question screener, and an answer counts when it falls in that question’s shaded band on the printed form. Part B carries no score at all — the form is explicit that “no total score or diagnostic likelihood is utilized” for those twelve.

What this is not

This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Only a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist can diagnose ADHD. A high score is a reason to book that conversation, not an answer; a low score does not rule anything out.

Nothing you answer here is stored or sent anywhere. There is no account, no analytics on your answers, and no copy kept — they exist in this tab while it is open and go when you close it.
