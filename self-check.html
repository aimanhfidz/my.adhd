Who wrote these questions

The eighteen questions in this self-check are the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-v1.1) Symptom Checklist. They are reproduced word for word; MyADHD Malaysia did not write them and has not altered them.

The checklist was developed in conjunction with the World Health Organization (WHO) and the Workgroup on Adult ADHD, which included:

  • Lenard Adler, MD — Associate Professor of Psychiatry and Neurology, New York University Medical School
  • Ronald C. Kessler, PhD — Professor, Department of Health Care Policy, Harvard Medical School
  • Thomas Spencer, MD — Associate Professor of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical School

The questions are consistent with DSM-IV criteria and address how ADHD symptoms show up in adults.

The scoring is the instrument’s own. Part A is the six-question screener, and an answer counts when it falls in that question’s shaded band on the printed form.

Part B carries no score at all. The form is explicit that “no total score or diagnostic likelihood is utilized” for those twelve.

What this is not

This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Only a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist can diagnose ADHD. A high score is a reason to book that conversation, not an answer; a low score does not rule anything out.

Your answers are stored, and this is what that means.

  • What is kept — your answers, your Part A score, and the details you gave: name, age, gender, and a phone number if you gave one.
  • Where — against your Google account, in our database at Supabase.
  • What for — three things and no others:
    • to follow up about ADHD support if you asked us to;
    • so you can find and delete your own record;
    • and, with your name and every other identifying detail stripped out, to count how Malaysians score.
  • For how long — 24 months after your last self-check, then it is deleted automatically.

This one is for adults.

The ASRS is the Adult self-report scale. It was written for people 18 and over, and its scoring has only ever been tested on them.

So a number from it would not mean anything for you. That is not a brush-off — it is the instrument being honest about what it is.

If focus, restlessness or getting started are hard right now, the person to talk to is a GP or a school counsellor. They can refer you to someone who assesses young people.
