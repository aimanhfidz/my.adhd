Living with ADHD · Rejection sensitivity

Why does a small no hurt this much?

You are not too sensitive. The sting is real.

The short version.

01

The sting is bigger than the moment

A short reply, a missed invite, a flat "ok". Others shrug it off in a minute. You carry it all afternoon, and it feels like proof of something.

02

Your brain fills the gap with the worst

No reply means busy, or asleep, or driving. But silence leaves a gap, and you fill it with the harshest story you know. Usually it is about you.

03

Notes on the work feel like notes on you

"Can you fix this part?" becomes "I am bad at this". The feedback was about one paragraph. The hurt was about everything.

04

So you please, pull back, or never ask

Say yes to everything. Quit before you can be turned down. Never ask at all. They look like different habits. They are the same shield.

05

Name it before you believe it

"This is the sting, not the facts." Saying it plainly puts a little space between the feeling and the story it is telling you.

06

Wait an hour before you reply

The urge to explain, apologise or cut them off is strongest in the first hour. Write the message if you must. Send it tomorrow, if it still makes sense.

07

Ask, instead of guessing

"Hey, was that ok?" is a small question. It costs far less than a night of rereading their last message for hidden meaning.

The feeling is loud, but it is not a verdict on you. Dump the spiral into my.adhd and get one thing back to do instead.
