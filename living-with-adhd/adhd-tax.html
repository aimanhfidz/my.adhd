Living with ADHD · The ADHD tax

The ADHD tax no one warned you about

It is not being bad with money. It is the price of a brain that forgets and acts fast.

The short version

01

Late fees for bills you could pay

The money was there. The reminder was not. A bill you can afford becomes one you pay twice, just because the date slipped past.

02

Buying it again

The charger, the umbrella, the tape. You own three somewhere. It is faster to buy a new one than to find the old one, so you do. Again.

03

The subscription you forgot

Free trial, never cancelled. The gym you visited twice. Small monthly amounts are easy to miss, and they add up quietly all year.

04

The 3am checkout

The rush of a new thing hits before the thinking does. Buy now, regret at delivery. The item was cheap. The pattern is not.

05

Put bills on autopay

Every payment that happens without you remembering is one less late fee. Set it up once, on a good day, and let the calendar do the work.

06

A 24-hour rule for wants

Leave it in the cart until tomorrow. If you still want it after a night's sleep, fine. Most urges do not survive the wait.

07

Check your statement monthly

Pick one day a month, put on a song, and scroll your bank statement. Cancel one thing you forgot you had. Five minutes, real money back.

You are not careless with money. You are paying for a busy brain. Dump the bills and dates into my.adhd and get one thing back to sort today.
