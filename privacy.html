Privacy

Last updated 13 September 2026

This is the whole of it, written to be read rather than to be survived. If anything below turns out not to match what the app does, the app is the bug and we want to hear about it. Most of my.adhd holds nothing of yours. The self-check is the exception, and it has a section of its own — with the same notice in Bahasa Melayu at the foot of this page.

The short version

  • Your tasks stay on your device unless you sign in. Signing in is optional and exists for one reason: so the same lists appear on your phone and your laptop. Do not sign in and there is no account, no login, and no copy of your lists anywhere but here.
  • The self-check is the one part that asks who you are. It needs a Google sign-in, your name, your age and your gender, and it keeps your answers — which are health data, so it asks you to agree to that separately and will not start until you have. Nothing else on the site works that way.
  • What you type into the dump box is sent to an AI to be turned into tasks, and is not stored afterwards. If you hold the mic and talk instead, the recording is sent to be transcribed — also not stored, and the microphone is only ever open while you are holding the button.
  • Google Calendar is off unless you turn it on, and when it is on, the app can only touch a calendar it made itself.
  • There is no tracking of any kind. No analytics, no advertising, no advertising profile, and nothing that follows you between sites.

Your tasks and your profile

Everything on your lists — the tasks, their dates, the steps, the name and face on your profile — lives in your browser's local storage on the device you typed it on. Signed out, that is the only place it is: not sent anywhere, not backed up, not readable by us. That is also the trade-off: clear your browser data and it is gone, and there is nothing we can do to bring it back.

Signing in, and what that changes

Signing in with Google is optional and the app never asks twice. In the app it buys two things: your lists appear on every device you sign in on, and the calendar link stops expiring. Nothing else about the app changes. On the self-check it does one more thing — it is what your screener record is attached to, so that you can come back and delete it.

To make the first of those work, a copy of your tasks is kept in our database at Supabase — the title, the date and time, the steps, and whether it is done. Each row is locked to your account by the database itself, so no other signed-in person can read it. Your local storage is still where the app reads from; the copy exists so the devices have somewhere to meet.

Your name, your chosen face, your energy setting and your calendar link are not part of that. They stay on each device separately. The self-check is the exception to all of this: it keeps its own record, in its own tables, under its own rules — see below.

Signing out stops the copying and leaves the lists on that device alone. Deleting your account deletes the copy with it, and there is a button for that on your profile: Delete account, under Sign out. It asks twice and then it is done — the account, the synced copy of your tasks, and the Google Calendar link all go together, and the refresh token we were holding is handed back to Google on the way out. Your self-check record goes with it too — the details you gave and every answer you saved. One thing outlives it, deliberately: the record that a consent was given, with your identity removed from it, so what is left says that somebody agreed, under which version of this notice and on what date, but not who. Nothing else is kept and there is nothing to undo it with.

What it does not touch is this device. The lists in your browser are still the lists in your browser; the account was only ever the place your devices met. If you want those gone too, Clear everything on the lists screen is the one that does it.

The dump box, and the AI that sorts it

When you press the button, the text you wrote is sent to our server and passed straight to Anthropic's API, which reads it and returns a structured list of tasks. That round trip is the feature; there is no way to have the sorting without it.

We do not keep the text. It is held in memory for the length of the request and not written to any database of ours. Anthropic processes it as our service provider, and does not train models on it. If the network is down or the AI is unreachable, the app falls back to a much simpler parser that runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing — you will see an "Offline mode" note when that happens.

Practically: treat the dump box the way you would treat any AI chat. It is a good place for "chase the landlord about the boiler" and a bad place for your passwords.

Hold to talk

The microphone is only ever open while your finger is on the button. There is no wake word, nothing listens in the background, and letting go stops the recording and releases the microphone — which is what puts out your device's recording indicator.

When you let go, the recording is sent to our server and passed straight to Google's Gemini API, which listens to it and returns the text. That is the feature: an engine that hears the audio is the only kind that can follow a sentence which starts in Malay and ends in English, or get a road name right. Along with the audio we send the names already on your own lists, so that the things you have written down before are spelled the way you spell them.

We do not keep the recording. It is held in memory for the length of the request and never written to disk or to any database of ours. Google processes it as our service provider on the paid API, which is not used to improve their models. The text that comes back goes into the dump box for you to read and edit — nothing is added to your lists until you press the button yourself.

If the transcriber cannot be reached, the app falls back to your browser's own speech engine and tells you that is what you are looking at. Note that in Chrome that engine also sends audio to Google; in Safari it runs on your device. Neither is as accurate, which is why it is the fallback.

The same rule as the dump box applies, with more force because talking is easier than typing: it is a good place for "chase the landlord about the boiler", and a bad place for anything you would not want transcribed.

Google Calendar

This is off until you link it, and the app works fully without it.

When you link it, the app asks Google for one narrow permission: calendar.app.created. That lets it create its own calendar, named my.adhd, and manage events on that one calendar. It cannot read, change or delete anything on the calendars you already had. This is enforced by Google, not merely promised here.

For each task that has a date, the app writes an event to that calendar containing the task's title, its first step, its rough duration, its category and its energy level. When you tick the task off, delete it, or clear your lists, the event is deleted too. Nothing is ever read back out of your calendar, so the app never learns anything about the rest of your diary.

The access token Google issues is stored in your browser, on your device, and is discarded the moment it expires — Google gives it one hour. We never see it: the calls go from your browser straight to Google and never pass through our servers, so there is no copy of it anywhere else.

It used to be held only in memory, which is safer, and it was changed deliberately. Renewing it invisibly requires a Google session that iPhones do not allow an app like this to reach, so on a phone the app ended up asking you to sign in again before it would save a single dated task. What made that trade acceptable is how little the token can do: it only ever reaches the my.adhd calendar, never the ones you already had.

Unlinking forgets the connection, deletes the stored token, and tells Google to revoke it. The events already in your calendar are left alone, and you can delete the my.adhd calendar yourself at any time from Google Calendar. You can also revoke the app's access from your Google account permissions page.

The self-check

The self-check is the ASRS-v1.1, a screening questionnaire developed with the World Health Organization. It is the one part of my.adhd that asks who you are, and the only part that keeps anything about your health. Everything in this section applies to it and to nothing else.

What it keeps. Your name, the email address on the Google account you signed in with, your age, your gender, a phone number if you give one — it is optional and the screener works the same without it — and your answers together with your Part A score.

Why it is treated differently. Answers about attention, restlessness and concentration are information about your health, and the PDPA calls that sensitive personal data. The law asks for explicit consent before anyone may process it, which is why the page puts that on a tick of its own rather than folding it into a general agreement, and why it will not start until you have ticked it.

What it is for. Three things, and the page names all three before it asks:

  • To follow up with you about ADHD support — only if you tick the third box, which is optional and separate.
  • So we know which record is yours, and you can ask to see it, correct it or delete it.
  • To count how Malaysians score, with your name and every other identifying detail stripped out. The counts are built so that a group of fewer than five people is never reported at all, because a small enough statistic is a person.

Who else sees it. Nobody outside MyADHD, except the providers that hold it for us — Supabase, our database, and Vercel, our host. It is not sold, not shared for advertising, and not given to an employer, an insurer or a school.

It is never obligatory. Close the page and nothing is kept. The consequence of not giving us this is simply that we cannot save your result or find it again for you — there is no other penalty, and the questions themselves are free to anyone.

The record is yours to end. Delete account in the app removes the details and every saved answer. We keep it for 24 months after your most recent self-check in any case, and then it is deleted automatically.

Feedback

If you send a note from the feedback screen, we store the text of it. We do not store your name, your email, or anything that identifies you, because the feedback box has never asked you for any of those. It is not connected to your self-check record and there is no key that would join the two.

We do store a one-way, salted hash of your IP address alongside it, for one reason: the box takes one note per person per day, and something has to count. The hash cannot be turned back into an address, it is never shown to anyone, and it is not used to link your notes to each other or to anything else. Notes are stored with Supabase, our database provider.

Hosting and logs

The site is hosted on Vercel, which keeps ordinary server logs — IP address, timestamp, which page was requested — as every web host does. We do not add anything to those logs, do not analyse them, and cannot use them to identify you.

What we do not do

  • No analytics. Not Google Analytics, not a privacy-friendly alternative, not a page-view counter. There is genuinely nothing.
  • No advertising, and no data sold, rented or shared with anyone for it.
  • No cookies of ours. The app stores things in your browser, and nothing we set is sent to a server. Signing in is the one exception, and even there the cookie is not ours: the sign-in hops through Google and Supabase, on their own domains, and whatever they set is set by them.
  • No third-party scripts on the page — except Google's own sign-in library, which is loaded only at the moment you press "Link Google Calendar", and never before. Signing in to the self-check does not load anything: it works by sending you to Google and back, which is a different thing from putting Google inside our page.
  • No profiling and no automated decisions about you. A self-check score is worked out by the instrument's own published rules, shown to you, and never used to decide anything without a person involved.

Children and young people

The app is not built or intended for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. It asks nobody their age, so there is no way for it to detect one — a limitation, stated plainly rather than dressed up.

The self-check asks outright, and stops at 18. Two reasons, and both matter. In Malaysia a person under 18 cannot give consent for themselves, and we have no way to verify a parent's. And the instrument is the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale: it was written for adults and its scoring has only ever been validated on them, so a number from it would not mean anything for a younger person anyway. Someone who tells us they are under 18 is shown where to get help instead, and nothing they typed is sent anywhere or kept: no name, no age, no answers, and no record of any kind in our database.

One thing about that is worth saying plainly, because the alternative is a promise with an asterisk on it. The screener asks you to sign in before it asks your age, so by the time somebody tells us they are under 18 a Google sign-in has already happened and an account exists at Supabase. We sign it straight back out and nothing is ever written against it — but the account was made, and we would rather write that here than claim the page never touches the network on that path. If you are under 18 and would like that account removed as well, write to hello@myadhd.my and we will delete it.

Your rights over your data

Access, correction, deletion, portability. For most of my.adhd these are unusual rights to exercise, because we mostly do not hold anything to exercise them against. For the self-check we hold a great deal, and there they matter in the ordinary way. In practice:

  • Your lists: yours already. "Clear everything" on the profile screen deletes them, as does clearing site data in your browser.
  • Your calendar events: unlink on the profile screen, then delete the my.adhd calendar in Google if you want the events gone as well.
  • A feedback note: we cannot find it by asking you who you are, because we do not know. Quote it back to us and we will delete it.
  • Your self-check record: the details you gave and every answer you saved. Write to us and we will send you a copy, correct anything that is wrong, or delete the lot — and Delete account in the app deletes it without asking us at all. If you ticked the box allowing us to contact you, say the word and we will stop; that one is a preference, not a condition, and withdrawing it does not take the screener away from you.

In Malaysia these rights are the ones the Personal Data Protection Act 2010 gives you, and if we get this wrong you can complain to the Personal Data Protection Commissioner (Jabatan Perlindungan Data Peribadi). If you are in the UK, the EU, or anywhere with similar law, you have the equivalent right to complain to your own authority.

Changes

If this changes in a way that affects what happens to what you type, the date at the top changes and the change is described here rather than quietly folded in.

How long we keep things

  • Your self-check record: 24 months after your most recent self-check, then deleted automatically. The deletion is a job that runs nightly inside the database, not a promise somebody has to remember to keep. Taking the screener again starts the 24 months over.
  • The consent record: the same 24 months, and it is stripped of your identity the moment you delete your account.
  • Your synced tasks: for as long as the account exists. Delete the account and they go with it.
  • What you type into the dump box, and anything you say into the mic: not kept at all. It exists for the length of one request.
  • A feedback note: kept until we have acted on it, and in practice longer — there is no name attached to it to expire.

Where your data is

Our database is Supabase and our host is Vercel. The AI that sorts a dump is Anthropic, and transcription is Google. All of them run on servers outside Malaysia, which means your data is transferred out of the country when you use those parts of the app. The PDPA allows that with your consent and where the receiving place gives comparable protection; we are telling you plainly rather than leaving it out.

Donations and Stripe

If you choose to give something, the payment is handled entirely by Stripe on their own pages. We never see or store your card. What we keep is what Stripe tells us afterwards: a customer id, which plan if any, whether it is current, and when it next renews — enough to know whether an account is entitled, and nothing about the card itself. That record goes when the account goes.

Contact

Questions, corrections, a copy of what we hold, or a note to delete: hello@myadhd.my. MyADHD is the data user for everything described on this page. We answer access and correction requests within 21 days, which is the period the PDPA sets; if we need longer we will tell you why before that runs out.
