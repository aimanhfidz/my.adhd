Why a box helps

Four things that are true about a head with too much in it.

  • A written plan quiets it as well as finishing does

    Interrupt someone mid-task and the task keeps interrupting back, crowding whatever they turned to instead. Writing a specific plan for it stopped that completely — while the task itself stayed undone.

    Masicampo & Baumeister, 2011

  • Your head holds about four things

    That is the working limit of short-term memory once you stop rehearsing it. A Tuesday has more than four things in it, so the list has to live somewhere that isn't you.

    Cowan, 2001

  • Time is the part that isn't visible

    Across 55 studies, people with ADHD were consistently off when judging how long something takes. So the minutes get written down like everything else — an estimate on every task, not a guess.

    Marx et al., 55-study meta-analysis

  • One first step, small enough to start

    Plans do the most work when they say when, where and how. So every task comes back with one move on the front of it: the smallest thing that counts as having started.

    Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006

None of this is treatment. It is what the research says about getting a thing out of your head and into a plan.
