Design principles

Six rules the whole system leans on.

None of these are aspirations. Each one is already enforced somewhere in the code, and the note under it says where.

01

Air, and one idea at a time.

Sections are separated by a whole band of nothing, and an opener takes the window to itself. A page that arrives all at once is the exact experience the product exists to argue against.

02

Three faces, three jobs, no overlap.

Baloo 2 sets display headings and the wordmark. DM Sans sets anything that is a sentence. DM Mono sets anything that is a label. A heading in the wrong face is the most common way this system breaks.

03

Colour is a signal, not decoration.

Orange means press this, or this is late — and nothing else, ever. Red means destructive. Both only work while nothing on the page competes for them, so near-monochrome is the ground they sit on.

04

A hairline grid, and one rule down the left.

Every page is a document with a left edge, not a centred column. A single 1px rule runs the height of the page and the content hangs off it.

05

Nothing invented.

No stock photography, no placeholder testimonials, no filler copy. Where a page needs content that does not exist yet, it gets a frame and a visible data-placeholder stamp that looks like scaffolding on purpose.

06

Both themes are the brand.

Dark is not a courtesy mode. Every colour in the system is a token, the night palette re-values them, and no component is ever allowed to name a colour directly.

The mark

An asterisk with one arm come loose.

Seven orange arms radiate from a centre. The eighth has detached and sits just off it as a violet capsule. That is the whole idea, and it is drawn once — in a 100-unit box, as a sprite every page reuses.

Construction

Geometry, 100-unit box

Four 7 × 64 bars crossed at 0°, 90°, 45° and −45°, the last one halved to 7 × 32 so its lower-right arm is missing. Cap radius 32 from centre.

The detached arm

A round-capped stroke from 64.5, 64.5 to 71.5, 71.5, weight 7. It is the only violet in the mark and it never changes colour.

Optical bearing

The ink spans 18–82, so the box carries an 18% bearing it does not use. A lockup set flush to a gutter therefore looks inset — give that bearing back at the other end of the row.

Colour

Near-monochrome, so two colours can mean something.

Vivid Blue is the accent and carries interactive chrome. Orange has exactly two jobs and red has exactly one. Everything else on a page is paper, ink and a hairline. Click any value to copy it.

Primary

Signal — spend these, don't decorate with them

Orange fills the circle of every primary button, and backs anything that is late — overdue, urgent, a sync gone stale. It appears in no heading, on no rule, and as no ornament. Red is destructive actions only. The two never swap jobs, because a person who has learned that orange means start here should not meet it meaning danger.

Secondary — the blue and violet ramps

Surfaces & ink · day

Surfaces & ink · night

Brand blue is far too dark to sit on #101018, so at night the accent lifts to #8B7DFF and text on top of it goes back to near-black. The violet deliberately does not move: WCAG 1.4.3 exempts logotypes from contrast minimums, and a violet that shifted at night would put a different purple in the nav from the one in the browser tab beside it.

Typography

One face for names, one for sentences, one for labels.

All three are self-hosted in production. A page that has to ask a third party before it can draw its own headline is a page with a flash of the wrong type in it.

Graphic elements

A rule, a circle, and the weather.

There is no component library and no icon set beyond the app's own. What exists is a small vocabulary, used consistently enough that it reads as one thing.

--radius for controls and rows, --radius-lg for panels, --radius-card for the app's cards, and full round for anything that is a button circle, a chip or a toggle.

Buttons — the only place colour fills a shape
Take the self-check Read about us

A label, then a circle. Primary fills the circle with orange at 56px and grows it to 62px on hover. Secondary is the same object at half volume: a 40px outlined circle for a link that continues rather than concludes. The arrow moves, the circle grows, nothing else happens. No pills, no filled rectangles, no drop shadows.

The wave field

Where a brochure would put a photograph, this system puts weather. The field is drawn from six named ramp values — a ground, a mid and a high, one set for paper and one for night — and it runs behind the page rather than inside a frame. Day runs pale-to-pale so nothing sitting over it picks up a halo; night runs near-black → deep blue → vivid blue under a flat wash. Toggle the theme to see both.

Destructive, and why it is a different red

Delete this list and everything in it?

#D92D20 carries white text at 4.8:1 on both grounds, so the fill does not move between themes. Red as text is the part that fails on a dark page, which is why night has a separate ink at #FF8A7E. Red is never a border on a valid field, never an accent, never an empty state.

Needs supplying

What this book cannot yet say.

Everything above is lifted from the repo. The items below are not specified anywhere in it, so they are listed rather than invented.

  • Print colour No CMYK or Pantone equivalents exist. The palette is screen-only, and Vivid Blue at #4737FF will not survive a four-colour press without a decision about what it becomes.
  • Horizontal / stacked lockups Only the horizontal lockup is drawn. A stacked version and a mark-with-organisation-name version would both be useful for square formats and print, and neither exists.
  • Monochrome lockup There is no one-colour or knockout version of the mark for embroidery, stamps, or a partner's logo strip.
  • Photography direction The system deliberately uses a wave field where a photograph would go. If photography is ever introduced, nothing currently says what it should look like.
  • An icon set Beyond the app tile and favicon, icons in the interface are drawn ad hoc. No grid, weight or terminal style is specified.
  • Bahasa Melayu type The site ships a BM switch, but nothing records whether the scale changes for Malay — which runs longer than English and may need the display clamps easing. The words are now written down (see below); the type is not.
  • Voice & tone, in English The copy has a consistent register — plain, unhurried, never clinical — but it has never been written down as a rule anyone else could follow. The Malay half now has been: docs/bahasa-melayu-voice.md sets the register at awak, bans the DBP words, and rules the ASRS questions and the consent copy out of scope. English still has no equivalent page.
