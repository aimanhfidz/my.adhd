# Taglines

Five approved lines, in three registers. They are not interchangeable: each one
makes a different promise, and the surface decides which promise is the right
one to make. Pick **one** per surface and let it stand alone — two taglines in
one view read as indecision, and a tagline stacked on a headline reads as
neither.

Type them exactly as written here, full stops and all. The full stop is the
rhythm; without it the line is a fragment, not a statement.

## Empowering

> **Different Minds. Limitless Potential.**

The widest line, and the least specific. It says nothing about what the product
does, which is what makes it right where the product is not the subject: the
top of a landing page, an event banner, a sponsor deck, a poster, the back of a
shirt. It is the line to reach for when the audience includes people who do not
have ADHD and are not about to sign up for anything.

Do not use it on a page whose next move is a signup. It raises the register and
then asks for an email, and the join reads as a bait.

## Modern

> **Understand Your Mind. Own Your Day.**

The product line. It names the actual arc of the app — the brain dump first,
then the one task — and it is the only one of the five that implies a tool
rather than a feeling. Use it where someone is about to decide whether to use
my.adhd: the app store listing, the meta description, the hero of a page with a
signup below it, a paid ad, the carousel outro when the CTA is the app.

It is also the safest one to put beside a screenshot, because it describes what
the screenshot is doing.

## Community-focused

Three lines, one register. They differ by how much warmth they spend.

> **Different Minds, One Community.**

The formal one, and the only tagline with a comma rather than two sentences —
it is a single claim, not a pair. Use it where the community is being named to
someone outside it: a group description, an invite link preview, a partnership
or a press line, the header of the community page itself.

> **Understand. Connect. Thrive.**

Three verbs, the tightest line of the five. It survives being small, so it is
the one for tight spaces — a bio, a footer, a badge, a sticker, the strip under
a logo. It is also the most generic of the three; do not spend it where there
is room for one of the others.

> **A Place for Minds Like Yours.**

The warmest, and the only line addressed to one person rather than a group. Use
it in the invitation itself — the join CTA, the carousel slide that asks
someone to come in, a DM reply, a welcome message. It works because it is small;
put it on a billboard and it goes soft.

## Rules

- **One line per surface.** Never two, never a tagline plus a sub-tagline.
- **The tagline is not a headline.** If the page already has a headline doing
  the persuading, the tagline goes in the footer or nowhere.
- **Sentence-final full stops stay.** `Understand. Connect. Thrive.` is three
  sentences on purpose.
- **Never translate one into Malay on the spot.** A BM surface gets a line
  written in BM to the rules in `bahasa-melayu-reference.md`, and it will not match
  the English one word for word. Until such a line exists and is written down
  here, a BM surface runs with no tagline rather than a translated one.
- **The brand name is `my.adhd`** in prose and `MYADHD` in tracked caps labels.
  A tagline does not change that, and never absorbs it — the line is not
  "my.adhd: Different Minds. Limitless Potential."
